# ACTIONS ON: {{CODENAME}}

*Frontmatter for the instantiated document also carries: cell G7; status ISSUED and what it feeds, being the operator brief, the run order and the battle inspection; date; h_hour {{H_HOUR}}; for {{HIGHER_COMMANDER}}, in the room, alone.*

Cards. Read once before you go in. Each is what you say, in your own words, when the thing happens. Each ends with the sentence that puts you back on the ask. Nothing here needs anyone else.

**These six are standing and are instantiated for every operation.** Add operation-specific cards after them where the estimate identified a branch the six do not cover. Do not remove one because it seems unlikely.

---

## 1. The screen fails, or the product will not open

Work down, out loud, without apologising twice.

*State the fallback ladder in the operator's own voice, as continuous prose, not a list: turn the device round; open the offline copy, which is the same thing and needs nothing; open the address in a browser; talk from the printed card, because everything on the product is on that card and you know where every figure came from.*

"None of this is the point. The point is {{ASK_QUESTION}}"

## 2. The key person is absent

I do not shrink the meeting to fit the room. I run the same demonstration, at the same length, to the people who are here, and I ask the same question of whoever is most senior. If nobody can answer it, I ask for the smaller thing instead: *the smaller ask, named.* I say once, plainly, that I would like to show {{ABSENT_PERSON_ROLE}} the same thing, and I ask when that could be. I do not send it over. *State what the product is and is not: a demonstration, not a document.*

"{{SMALLER_ASK}}"

## 3. An unknown senior attendee

I find out who they are before I start, by asking. Then I run it for them, because they have the shorter clock and they will decide whether this survives the retelling. I answer them first when they speak, and I keep it to three sentences. *If they ask what it is for, the answer, in one sentence, framed as an open decision rather than as a failure on the subject's part.* I do not upgrade the ask because a more senior person walked in.

"{{ASK_QUESTION}}"

## 4. A question I cannot answer

I do not guess and I do not talk around it. I say it plainly, I take the name of the person asking, and I put a time against it.

"I don't know, and I won't guess. You will have it by {{TIME}}."

Then I write it down where they can see me write it, and I move on. One sentence, no apology, no theory offered in the meantime. Guessing in this room costs more than not knowing does, and every figure on the product has a source I can show. If they press, I say the same words again.

"Back to the thing I came for: {{ASK_QUESTION}}"

## 5. A component breaks, or a source is stale

I say what it is, on the component, and I keep going.

"That one is a snapshot. It was taken at {{TIME_ON_THE_COMPONENT}} and it is not moving."

Then the why, because the why is the argument: *one sentence on what the product rests on and why that thing goes down sometimes.* If it is broken rather than stale, I say so and move on. I do not reload it twice in front of them. A picture that breaks honestly is worth more than one that never breaks, and I can say where every figure came from.

"Which is a good reason to fund somebody to own it. {{ASK_QUESTION}}"

## 6. The room has no connectivity

I say it once, at the start, before anyone notices.

"There is no connection in here, so everything you are looking at is a snapshot with the time it was taken printed on it. Nothing is refreshing."

Then I say nothing more about it. Every component carries its own time and its own source, so the demonstration is exactly the same demonstration. I do not hunt for a hotspot, I do not ask about guest access, and I do not offer to come back and show them the live version. It is the same picture with a timestamp on it.

"So: {{ASK_QUESTION}}"

## {{N}}. {{OPERATION_SPECIFIC_BRANCH}}

*One card per additional branch the estimate identified. Same shape: what I do, the words I say in quotation marks, the one-sentence why, then the ask. Two branches recur often enough to be worth naming as candidates: someone asks for a number, and someone asks whether this is a product they can buy today. Both are written as cards below and are kept where the operation has an ask or a product question.*

## {{N}}. Someone asks for a number

I do not give one, and I say why in one sentence rather than deflecting.

"I will not guess at that in front of you, because whatever I say is the number you will remember."

Then the honest answer: *what produces the price, and what the buyer gets for it, named as artefacts they own.* If they press for a range, I say the same words again.

"What I need first is {{ASK_QUESTION}}"

## {{N}}. Is this a product we can buy today

*Answer straight away, in one sentence.*

"This is {{PRODUCT}}. It is our engine, built by us. It is not a product on a price list and I am not selling you a licence today."

*Then the line: what is marked live is running now with its source and its time on it; what is marked designed is what the money buys and is not built.*

"So the question is {{ASK_QUESTION}}"

---

**Standing rules, all cards.** Say the honest thing before they find it. Concede what is true, first and fast. Never guess a number, a figure or a date. Never claim a person or a team MITMARK does not have. Every card ends on the ask, and after the ask, stop talking.
