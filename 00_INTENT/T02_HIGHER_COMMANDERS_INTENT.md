### A1. Context

"What keeps me up at night" is the revenue into MITMARK Intelligence. We currently sell DPO and vCISO support as a human service to one retained client. They pay a retainer for it. Securing that income against a sceptical CFO matters more than trying to sell the service anywhere else. These are lean times; the contract must be made sticky and be renewed.

The person who decides whether it is sticky is the client's Information Officer: a part-time role carried by a full-time employee. Rich sees her as key to keeping the contract.

Sunray: the renewal date is not the driver. Rich is driving the demand. The key is showing the value add and the cost save.

Prior work exists (a twelve-line vDPO service definition, an entry-engagement proposal, an unfinished compliance platform) but none of it was built for this client or this person.

### A2. Purpose

Near: the Information Officer feels, sees and can articulate the time and effort MITMARK saves her, in terms her CFO accepts, and the cost that saving represents.
Far: the retainer renews, and the same evidence becomes the case for the next client.
If only one can be had, the near thing wins. The argument the product has to win is a value-for-money argument put by a non-specialist to a sceptic.

### A3. Method

Governing fact: the product's user is one part-time Information Officer with a full-time job elsewhere, and its audience is her sceptical CFO.

Consequences:
- Anything that adds to her workload fails. It must take work off her, not give her a new system to feed.
- The saving must be visible as a record, not a claim: what MITMARK did, when, how long it would have taken her, what it cost to have MITMARK do it instead, what it prevented.
- Output has to be something she can forward to the CFO unedited.
- The human service continues. This sits under it and makes it legible; it does not replace the people the client is paying for.
- MITMARK review before anything reaches the client stays mandatory.
- Sunray: all twelve service lines are assessed and their delivery prioritised in a way that supports the intent; the beta shows the whole catalogue with the live and high-value lines first, not a subset.
- Sunray: the beta is delivered at £0, with a clear signal of where modest spend would improve it.

### A4. Main effort

The Information Officer's own account of what MITMARK saves her, in time and in money. When building the platform competes with making her account true and sayable, her account wins.

### A5. End state

At 23:59 on 16 Sep 2026:

After H-hour, not tested on the day: she has said in her own words what MITMARK saves her, and that is written down; the CFO has seen the record before the renewal decision.

### A7. What I will not accept

- A new system she has to log into and maintain herself.
- Claims of value with no record behind them.
- Anything that reads as MITMARK marketing to the CFO.
- The human service being visibly thinned to pay for this.
- The client's name in any document, URL or screen that can be forwarded.
- Spend on the beta.

### A8. Standing guidance

Would the Information Officer say this makes her job easier and her case to the CFO stronger, and could she put a number on it? If not, do not build it.
