# T02A: INTENT CRAFT GUIDE

## Why this exists

One intent document this method has produced took sustained, deliberate effort to get right.
That standard should not depend on how much time Sunray has on a given day. Nine questions,
asked once, in the order below, do most of the work a blank T02 currently asks Sunray to do
alone. Seagull drafts T02 from the answers. Sunray corrects the draft. The labour of
construction moves to Seagull; the judgement stays with Sunray.

Take every answer as given, however rough, incomplete or bluntly worded. Do not soften,
generalise or tidy an answer while it is being given. The roughness is often the honest part.

## The nine questions

**1. Context.** What is the honest position right now? Say it the way you would say it to
someone you trust completely, not the way you would write it up. What do you have that almost
nobody else your size has, and how long does it stay open?

**2. Purpose, near and far.** What do you want to be true afterwards? Say the near thing you
are actually asking for, then the far thing behind it, if there is one. If you can only get
one, which matters more?

**3. The one governing fact.** Is there one fact about how this has to be run, who does it,
alone or not, under what condition, that changes everything else about how it is built? Name
it. Every other method constraint should be worked out from that fact, not invented
separately.

**4. Main effort and its tie-break.** If everything else about this fails but one thing
succeeds, what is that one thing? And when two good options compete for time, money or
attention, which one wins, always?

**5. End state, testable.** List what has to be true when this is over. Each line should be
something a stranger could check by watching or looking, not something only you would know to
ask about.

**6. Freedoms.** What do you not want to be asked about again before this is finished? Say it
plainly. That is what makes delegation real rather than nominal.

**7. What I will not accept.** Name three to five ways this could go wrong that you would
regret. Short, declarative, one per line. Things, not feelings.

**8. Standing guidance.** When nobody knows what to do and you are not there to ask, what is
the one question that would settle it? Tie it to what actually matters, not to a general
principle.

**9. Ratification and notes.** Will the higher commander see a draft of this, or is this the
only chance to get it right? Is there anything you know that was not said, that the build
needs to know?

## Drafting T02 from the answers

Seagull writes A1 to A9 in the commander's own words wherever the answer supplied them, per
T02's own usage note. Question 3's answer governs the whole of A3: every method constraint
written there should be shown as a consequence of that one fact, not listed independently.
Question 4's answer becomes A4 exactly: the main effort sentence, then its tie-break, with
nothing else added. Question 7's answer is not expanded or explained; each line stands alone,
the way A7 elsewhere in this pack does.

## The seven-line check, before it goes back to Sunray

Seagull runs this against its own draft before presenting it. A failure names the specific
line; it is never returned as a general instruction to make it better.

1. Does A1 state the honest position in one place, without softening?
2. Does A2 separate a near thing from a far thing, in one sentence?
3. Does every constraint in A3 trace back to the one governing fact, rather than reading as an
   invented list?
4. Does A4 carry its own tie-break for when two good things compete?
5. Is every line in A5 something a stranger could check by looking, not by asking?
6. Is A7 three to five short declarative lines, each independently testable, none of them a
   feeling rather than a thing?
7. Does A8 give one rule for the unanticipated case, tied to what actually matters?

A draft that passes all seven goes to Sunray as a draft for correction. A draft that fails any
of them is redrafted before Sunray sees it; his time is spent correcting judgement, not fixing
construction.
